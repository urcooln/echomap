Enhance Pattern Context and Flexibility Tracking
Done looks like
Each recurring pattern displays a Context Variability Indicator showing the number of distinct settings, activities, communication partners, and reviewed sessions in which the pattern has been observed.
Patterns can be expanded to show a chronological timeline of verified observations.
The detail view highlights whether the pattern appears:
as an exact phrase,
within longer phrases,
at the beginning of phrases,
in the middle of phrases,
at the end of phrases.
Clinicians can view all dictionary phrases containing the pattern and sort by most recent, most frequent, or oldest observed.
The system identifies whether a pattern appears across multiple communication functions when those functions have already been clinician-confirmed elsewhere.
A pattern may display observational labels such as:
Used across multiple settings
Used across multiple communication partners
Appears in several phrase forms
Observed across multiple sessions
Recently increasing
These labels are descriptive only and must not imply developmental progress, NLA stage, mitigation status, gestalt status, diagnosis, treatment effectiveness, or readiness for intervention.
Additional Safety Requirements
Pattern frequency must never inflate counts through duplicate transcript entries, merged phrases, or repeated processing runs.
Deleted, archived, ignored, or clinician-rejected phrases must not contribute to recurring-pattern calculations.
All recurring-pattern outputs must be traceable back to underlying reviewed evidence through a clinician-accessible evidence view.
Future-Proofing
Store recurring-pattern observations separately from interpretation layers so future NLA, AAC, communication-function, and longitudinal analytics can reuse the same evidence without recalculating fragment relationships.
Preserve a stable pattern identifier so longitudinal tracking remains consistent as additional reviewed language is added.
The single most valuable addition is the Context Variability Indicator. Clinicians care less that a fragment occurred 30 times and more that it occurred:
at home,
at school,
during play,
during regulation,
with different partners,
across multiple sessions.
That turns "frequent phrase" into a much more meaningful communication pattern tracker while staying observational and evidence-based.
