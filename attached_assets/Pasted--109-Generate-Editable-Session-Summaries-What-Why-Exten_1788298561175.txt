#109 - Generate Editable Session Summaries

What & Why

Extend ChildLed’s post-review workflow with a structured, editable clinical Session Summary grounded only in reviewed evidence. The summary should help clinicians understand what was observed, what changed, and what may warrant follow-up without turning AI output into an autonomous clinical conclusion.

Done looks like

After Child Language Review, an authorized clinician can generate and edit a structured Session Summary.
The summary includes clearly separated sections for Observed Language, Communication Functions Observed, Notable Language Changes, NLA / Gestalt Insights, Session Participation, AAC Planning Opportunities, Suggested Dictionary Candidates, and Suggested Follow-Up Targets.
Observed Language uses direct examples from clinician-confirmed Child utterances and distinguishes repeated, novel, longer, functional, possible gestalt, and emerging-combination language when supported.
Communication functions include only requesting, commenting, protesting, directing, assistance seeking, social interaction, information sharing, self-advocacy, or regulation/support seeking when reviewed evidence supports them.
Changes compare the current reviewed session with prior reviewed sessions, dictionary history, and previously observed phrases using cautious, non-definitive wording.
Gestalt and NLA content is advisory only, uses wording such as “may reflect” or “could indicate,” and never assigns a stage as fact.
AAC candidates, dictionary candidates, and follow-up considerations remain suggestions requiring clinician action; they do not automatically update AAC planning, the dictionary, treatment plans, or the medical record.
Session participation is limited to transcript evidence and session metadata, with no invented engagement, intent, or responsiveness claims.
The draft is editable, survives reload, and requires explicit authenticated clinician review and save before becoming persisted clinical documentation.
Every generated statement is traceable to the reviewed utterances, phrase candidates, dictionary entries, or prior-session evidence that contributed to it, with expandable source details for clinicians.
The summary distinguishes directly observed language, longitudinal comparisons, and AI-generated interpretations; interpretation sections visibly say “AI-generated clinical observation” and “Clinician review required.”
A Communication Growth Snapshot summarizes supported new phrases, repeated phrases, new functions, reused phrases in new contexts, emerging flexibility or recombination, and meaningful frequency changes using cautious language.
An optional Family & Team Highlights section provides 3–5 plain-language observations suitable for caregiver or educational-team adaptation, excludes NLA and diagnostic language, requires review, and never sends automatically.
Session-to-session continuity identifies newly observed phrases, phrases not observed this session, increasing or decreasing reviewed frequency, and first-observed communication functions when prior reviewed sessions exist.
Empty, incomplete, unsupported, provider-error, cross-child, cross-organization, and unauthorized cases fail explicitly without generating unsupported content.
API, persistence, and browser coverage verifies Child-only evidence, longitudinal comparisons, edit/save behavior, required labels, privacy isolation, and prohibited diagnostic or treatment language.
Out of scope

Diagnoses, definitive NLA stage assignment, treatment recommendations, therapy goals, or autonomous clinical decisions.
Treating Not Child, Unsure, Unintelligible, pending inbox items, raw transcript text, or unconfirmed phrases as evidence.
Automatically adding phrases to the Communication Dictionary or AAC Planning.
Automatically sharing Family & Team Highlights with caregivers, educators, or other team members.
Replacing the existing Child Language Review, SOAP workspace, Communication Passport, or team-messaging workflows.
Exporting to an external EHR or introducing a new sharing workflow.
Steps

Define the structured summary contract -- Specify the evidence-backed sections, cautious labels, expandable source references, growth snapshot, shareable highlights, empty states, and editable fields so each output is distinguishable from a finalized clinical record.
Assemble reviewed longitudinal context -- Combine the current session’s confirmed Child evidence with approved dictionary history, prior reviewed phrases, child profile context, interests, sensory/regulation supports, session metadata, and supported communication-function evidence while preserving provenance, comparison windows, and exclusions.
Generate and validate the draft -- Produce constrained structured output through the existing AI boundary, reject unsupported or incomplete provider output, and visibly label generated interpretation as draft and clinician-review required.
Expose evidence and sharing boundaries -- Let clinicians expand summary content to inspect its reviewed sources and edit plain-language Family & Team Highlights without mixing shareable text into internal clinical interpretation or sending anything automatically.
Add clinician editing and explicit save -- Persist the editable draft separately from finalized notes, preserve edits across reload and regeneration, and require an authorized clinician transition before saving clinical documentation.
Verify privacy and safety -- Add endpoint, persistence, and browser tests for evidence traceability, Child-only evidence, longitudinal comparisons, growth and sharing sections, empty/partial review, provider failures, organization and child isolation, edit/save behavior, and prohibited claims.
